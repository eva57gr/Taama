Part 1 — The Decision Tree
How a product is classified in Australia, from “what is it?” down to a terminal category. Confirm the tree, then the categories and sources below.
├── FOOD (FSANZ Food Regime)- FSANZ
│      ├── General food — FSC Chapter 1
│      ├── Food products - FSC Chapter 2
│      ├── Special Purpose Food — Std 2.9.
│      ├── Caffeine products - 2.9.4—11
│      └── Dietary supplement (NZ only, dosed form) — NZ 1985 Regs
└── SUPPLEMENTS (TGA Complementary Medicines)
Guidance
├── AUST L(A) — Assessed Listed, pre-market efficacy review - Guidance
└── AUST R — Registered, full quality/safety/efficacy dossier - Guidance
Reclassification works like a simple grid: what the ingredient is allowed to do, combined with how strong the claim on the label is.
If the ingredients are all approved and the claim is low-key, the product lands in the basic AUST L category. If the ingredients are approved but the claim is a bit stronger, it moves up to AUST L(A). If the ingredients are approved but the claim is a strong one, it gets bumped up to the more heavily regulated AUST R category. And if any ingredient isn't approved at all, the product goes straight to AUST R regardless of what the claim says.

1.2 — Food vs Supplement: the split test
This is the criteria catalog that decides the very first fork — food (FSANZ) vs supplement/medicine (TGA). The two sides are shown together on purpose: these criteria ARE the split test. Claims are determinative — a therapeutic indication forces the TGA side regardless of form.

# | Criterion | Points toward FOOD (FSANZ) | Points toward SUPPLEMENT (TGA) | Priority | Source
1 | Intended use | Nutrition, sustenance, enjoyment | Therapeutic use (TG Act s 3) | Primary | Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 / Food-Medicine Interface Guidance Tool (FMIGT)
2 | Dosage form / presentation | Conventional food forms (beverage, bar, meal, snack) | Tablets, pills, capsules, pastilles, powders, liquids “and the like” | Primary | Food-Medicine Interface Guidance Tool (FMIGT)
3 | Claims | Nutrition content / health claims (FSANZ S4, NPSC-gated) | Indications (therapeutic claims) — any level forces the medicine side | Primary | Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 / FSC Std 1.2.7 (nutrition & health claims)
4 | Active ingredient concentration | Levels consistent with normal dietary intake | Megadose levels; scheduling thresholds (e.g. vitamin D) | Primary | Poisons Standard (SUSMP)
5 | Recommended daily dose | Grams / typical food portions | mg, IU, unit-dose regimen | Secondary | Food-Medicine Interface Guidance Tool (FMIGT)
6 | Target population | General population | Specific condition / physiological state | Secondary | Food-Medicine Interface Guidance Tool (FMIGT)
7 | Route of administration | Oral consumption as food | Oral, sublingual, topical, other routes (route scoping) | Secondary | TG (Permissible Ingredients) Determination
8 | Ingredient permissibility | All ingredients permitted for food (FSC) | Ingredients on the Permissible Ingredients list (Sch 14 designated actives) | Primary | TG (Permissible Ingredients) Determination / Proprietary Ingredients
9 | Manufacturing / GMP standard | Food safety / HACCP | PIC/S GMP (TG Act Part 3-3, Manufacturing Principles) | Secondary | Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 / Manufacturing principles (PIC/S GMP)
10 | Registration / notification | No pre-market approval (FSC compliance) | ARTG entry required before supply (AUST L / L(A) / R) | Secondary | Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 / Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG)

Is this split test correct & complete?   Yes, there are just several exceptions (mostly sports foods and powdered beverages)
1.3 — How a supplement lands in a TGA tier (ingredient × claim)
The tier is set by two axes: whether every ingredient is permissible, and how strong the strongest claim is. Any non-compliant ingredient jumps straight to AUST R regardless of the claim.

Ingredients \ Claim | Low-level claim | Intermediate claim | High-level claim
All ingredients permissible | AUST L | AUST L(A) | AUST R
Any non-compliant ingredient | AUST R | AUST R | AUST R

Is this grid correct?      ☐ No — correction: AUSTL (A) involves having an ‘assessed’ claim added to a product. It can be a higher level claim too, it just needs to be based on current permissible ingredients. This category is still relatively new. AUSTR was historically the higher level registered category that could involve adding specific claims from clinical research. It can have unique ingredients not available to AUSTL / AUSTL(A). These are not ‘non-compliant,’ they are just not assessed and approved for AUSTL medicines.

TGA-1  ·  SUPPLEMENT · TGA
AUST L — Listed Medicine (baseline supplement tier)
What this category is.  A self-certified complementary medicine in dosed form that uses ONLY pre-approved permissible ingredients within their limits and carries ONLY low-level permitted indications. Default supplement tier. Examples: daily multivitamin, fish-oil capsule, echinacea tablet.
A product is in this category when:  All actives AND excipients on the Permissible Ingredients list within dose limits · no scheduled poison (S2–S10) · every claim an exact / variation / semantic match to a low-level permitted indication · dosed form.

TGA-2  ·  SUPPLEMENT · TGA
AUST L(A) — Assessed Listed Medicine
What this category is.  A listed medicine that additionally carries at least one intermediate indication, supported by an efficacy evidence package the TGA assesses pre-market. Ingredients still restricted to the permissible list; label reviewed. May display the ‘TGA assessed’ claimer.
A product is in this category when:  Ingredients qualify exactly as for AUST L, BUT at least one indication is intermediate (not an exact/variation/semantic match to the permitted list) and is backed by a submitted evidence package.

TGA-3  ·  SUPPLEMENT · TGA
AUST R — Registered Medicine
What this category is.  Full pre-market evaluation of quality, safety and efficacy (CTD Modules 1–5). Reached when a supplement uses a non-compliant ingredient OR makes a high-level (serious-disease) indication. May use scheduled ingredients EXCEPT S4/S8/S9.
A product is in this category when:  Any non-compliant ingredient (not on the list / over limit / scheduled poison S2–S8 / restricted botanical) OR a high-level indication about a serious form of a disease. Prohibited representations remain banned at ALL tiers.
