3 Authority

This instrument is made under section 26BF of the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989.

4 Definitions

Note: A number of expressions used in this instrument are defined in subsection 3(1) of the Act, including the following:

(a) health practitioner;

(b) indications;

(c) label;

(d) medicine;

(e) presentation;

(f) Register;

(g) Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code.

In this instrument:

Act means the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989.

code tables means the tables accessed via the Code Tables item in the Public TGA Information menu in TGA eBusiness Services, as in force or existing from time to time.

dentist means a person registered as a dentist under a law of a State or Territory that provides for the registration of dentists.

doctor means a medical practitioner.

evidence guidelines means the document titled Supporting claims and indications for listed medicines, published by the Therapeutic Goods Administration, as in force or existing from time to time.

Note: The evidence guidelines are published at www.tga.gov.au.

health professional means a person mentioned in paragraph 42AA(a), (aa), (c) or (d) of the Act.

medically diagnosed, in relation to a disease, ailment, defect or injury, means a disease, ailment, defect or injury that is diagnosed by a suitably qualified medical practitioner.

medical practitioner means a person registered as a medical practitioner under a law of a State or Territory that provides for the registration of medical practitioners.

Regulations means the Therapeutic Goods Regulations 1990.

scientific, in relation to the type of evidence for an indication, means evidence that is based on scientific research and quantifiable data, as described in the evidence guidelines.

Note: Scientific evidence includes relevant clinical trials on humans and systematic reviews.

serious, in relation to a form of a disease, ailment, defect or injury, has the same meaning as in section 28 of the Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code, in relation to a form of a disease, condition, ailment or defect.

TGA eBusiness Services means TGA eBusiness Services on the Therapeutic Goods Administration’s website which may be accessed on the internet at www.ebs.tga.gov.au.

Therapeutic Goods Administration, or TGA, has the same meaning as in the Regulations.

traditional, in relation to the type of evidence for an indication, means evidence of traditional use in a recognised paradigm outside modern conventional medicine, as described in the evidence guidelines.

Note: Traditional evidence is a term of broad application and applies to different traditional paradigms, for example, traditional Ayurvedic medicine, traditional Chinese medicine and Western herbal medicine.

traditional Ayurvedic medicine, in relation to the type of evidence for an indication, means evidence that is based on traditional use in the Ayurvedic medicine paradigm.

traditional Chinese medicine or TCM, in relation to the type of evidence for an indication, means evidence that is based on traditional use in the traditional Chinese medicine paradigm.

traditional use has the same meaning as in the Regulations.

5 Permissible indications

The following indications are covered by this instrument under paragraph 26BF(1)(a) of the Act:

(a) each indication specified in column 2 of the tables in Parts 1 to 17 of Schedule 1; and

(b) each indication specified in column 2 of the tables in Parts 1 to 17 of Schedule 1, as modified by the use of one or more qualifying statements set out in the code tables under one of the following headings:

(i) traditional context qualifiers;

(ii) population qualifiers;

(iii) time of use qualifiers;

(iv) in relation to an indication that is based on TCM evidenceTCM pattern qualifiers.

Note: The population qualifiers set out in the code tables do not include pregnant individuals. Indications relating to pregnancy or pregnant populations are specified in Part 17 of Schedule 1.

6 Requirements in relation to permissible indications

(1) The requirements in this section are specified in relation to an indication covered by this instrument under paragraph 26BF(1)(b) of the Act.

(2) In relation to a medicine:

(a) the indication specified in column 2 of an item in a table in Parts 1 to 17 of Schedule 1 (the relevant indication) may only be used for the medicine if the relevant indication is supported by evidence of the type that is specified in column 3 of the item; and

(b) the requirements, if any, in relation to the relevant indication, set out in column 4 of the item must be met.

(3) The only indications that may be used in relation to a medicine that is a sunscreen product are the indications specified in column 2 of the table in Part 16 of Schedule 1.

Note: For the avoidance of doubt, the requirements in subsection (3) for medicines that are sunscreen products apply in addition to the requirements in subsection (2) for medicines.

(4) If the wording of an indication is varied on the label of a medicine, then the indication, as varied, must not:

(a) change the meaning or intent of the indication as specified in column 2 of an item in a table in Parts 1 to 17 of Schedule 1; and

(b) infer or imply that the medicine is for the treatment of a serious form of a disease, ailment, defect or injury; and

(c) infer or imply that the medicine is for preventing or curing a disease, ailment, defect or injury.

(5) Subject to subsection (6), if two or more indications are combined on the label of a medicine to form simple sentences where appropriate in the circumstances, then the indication, as combined, must not:

(a) change the meaning or intent of each indication as specified in column 2 of an item in a table in Parts 1 to 17 of Schedule 1; and

(b) infer or imply that the medicine is for the treatment of a serious form of a disease, ailment, defect or injury; and

(c) infer or imply that the medicine is for preventing or curing a disease, ailment, defect or injury.

(6) An indication specified in column 2 of an item in the table in Part 17 of Schedule 1 must not be combined on the label of a medicine with an indication specified in column 2 of an item in a table in Parts 1 to 16 of Schedule 1.

Note: For the avoidance of doubt, where appropriate, indications specified in column 2 of an item in the table in Part 17 of Schedule 1 and indications specified in column 2 of a table in Parts 1 to 16 of Schedule 1 may be listed separately on the label of a medicine. However, these indications must not be combined on the label to form simple sentences.

(7) If an indication in relation to a medicine is modified by one or more qualifying statements in accordance with paragraph 5(b) (whether or not the indication is varied or combined as described in subsection (4) or (5)), then:

(a) each qualifying statement must be set out on the label of the medicine; and

(b) the indication, as modified, varied or combined, must not infer or imply that a medicine is for the treatment of a serious form of a disease, ailment, defect or injury.

(8) If an indication in relation to a medicine is supported by traditional evidence, then:

(a) the indication must be qualified with an appropriate traditional context qualifier as set out in the code tables; and

(b) the traditional context qualifier mentioned in paragraph (a) must be set out on the label of the medicine.

7 Repeals

Each instrument that is specified in Schedule 2 to this instrument is repealed as set out in the applicable items in that Schedule.
